01 · Before

The Athabasca valley, summer 2024

Jasper sits where three valleys meet, ringed by peaks and wrapped in lodgepole pine. This block is about 43 by 46 km of it, from Pyramid Mountain in the north to Athabasca Falls in the south.

Years of mountain pine beetle had left large stands of dead, dry pine along the valley floor. By late July the forest was as dry as it gets.

Step through the satellite passes along the bottom. Until 22 July the valley barely changes. Then jump to 1 August.

02 · 22–24 July

Two days to the townsite

Fires started north and south of town on the evening of 22 July. Wind drove the southern fire up the valley, and two days later it reached Jasper.

  1. 22 Jul, 7:02 pmFire reported north of town, near the Jasper Transfer Station
  2. ~7:10 pmA second fire near Kerkeslin Campground, south of town; more follow nearby
  3. OvernightGusts up to 87 km/h merge the southern fires and push them north. Over 3,000 ha burn within hours
  4. 22–23 JulMore than 25,000 residents, workers and visitors evacuated
  5. 24 Jul, ~6 pmThe fire climbs the Whistlers' slopes; embers fall on the southwest corner of town
  6. 17 Aug · 7 SepDeclared held, then under control

03 · 24 July, evening

What stood in the way

358 of Jasper's 1,113 structures were lost. Crews saved the other 70%, including the hospital, the schools and the wastewater plant.

Burned ground here means forest and open land whose burn index dropped sharply between 20 July and 5 September, read at Sentinel-2's full 10 m resolution. The worst losses were on the west and southwest edges, where the forest runs right up to the houses.

Distance to burned ground measures exposure, not damage: a house can burn with no scorched forest next to it, and roofs barely register in the burn index. Building outlines are from OpenStreetMap.

Data and method

Imagery is Sentinel-2 Level-2A surface reflectance, resampled to a 1,024 px grid (~42 m). Burn is measured with the Normalized Burn Ratio, NBR = (NIR − SWIR) / (NIR + SWIR): a median of six clear passes before the fire is compared with a median of four after it (dNBR), and pixels with dNBR above 0.27 count as burned. Everything is built by the Python scripts in pipeline/, and the full analysis is in analysis.ipynb.
